# Same-machine benchmark on Quantinuum H2

Moses et al. prepared a 32-ion GHZ state on H2, rotated the measurement basis, and collected 32 fluorescence bits per shot. Quantinuum released the exact OpenQASM for each setting alongside its ordered measurement strings. This benchmark uses all 1,600 shots from the 32 nonadaptive phase settings. The other 32 computational-basis runs serve a different measurement and are excluded from the parity score. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.13.041052) · [Pinned physical shots and circuits](https://github.com/Quantinuum/quantinuum-hardware-h2-benchmark/blob/d422a716977899d7d6603c9efadb44e89e495125/ghz/data/GHZ_N32.json)

## Phase, measured parity and data split

The artifact applies a Hadamard on ion 0 followed by a 31-CNOT binary tree. At phase index k = 1, …, 32, each ion receives Rz((4 − k/32)π), then Ry(3.5π), then computational-basis readout. Removing the 4π rotation periods gives Rz(−θ) then Ry(−π/2), with θ = kπ/32. The measured single-ion observable is cos(θ)X + sin(θ)Y. Its product on the ideal GHZ state has expectation cos(32θ). Each recorded binary string contributes +1 for an even number of ones or −1 for an odd number. Their mean gives the observed parity at that angle. [Source circuit and shots](https://github.com/Quantinuum/quantinuum-hardware-h2-benchmark/blob/d422a716977899d7d6603c9efadb44e89e495125/ghz/data/GHZ_N32.json)

The reference controller sends two signed angle words through RAM, DMA, FIFO and DAC conversion. The response model recovers the digital angle words from the delivered values and evaluates the fitted offset plus the in-phase contrast times cos(32θ). The generic 10 ns slots and ±1 V mapping encode the two control words. Physical H2 optical pulse durations, voltages and electronics transfer functions remain unmeasured by these shot records. Studio represents the Z operation by a virtual phase-frame update and the Y operation by an equatorial pulse.

The split and threshold were fixed from the input circuits before parity reduction. All settings fall at extrema where sin(32θ) = 0. An alternating-singleton split would place just one sign in calibration and the other sign in testing. We therefore reserve alternating adjacent **pairs**, with indices congruent to 1 or 2 modulo 4 used for calibration and the remaining indices held out. Each partition has eight positive and eight negative ideal extrema. These data identify an offset and in-phase contrast. An independent phase lag, fringe shape between extrema, and drift between runs would require additional measurement settings.

The acceptance criterion is an unweighted held-out parity RMSE of at most 0.15. For 50 independent ±1 shots at a setting, the maximum standard error of the mean is 1/√50 = 0.1414. The criterion is an engineering tolerance comparable to this upper bound. The calibration gives an offset of −0.0025 and in-phase contrast of 0.7475. Across the 16 held-out phases (800 shots), RMSE is **0.08522**. The largest absolute residual is **0.185** at phase 24, where the model predicts +0.745 and 50 shots yield +0.56. This validates a calibrated same-H2 parity endpoint at the sampled settings. The shot files contain no timing observations for the controller or ion motion.

## Native source binding

Each phase circuit has the same preparation and final bit mapping as the Z-basis GHZ circuit used in QCCD Studio's published H2 reproduction. Removing only the analysis gates recovers that source byte for byte, including the barriers. Its SHA-256 is `f51e7c0493bfd29db3efc60a0b8fbedb6817627ff3c23aaa406e173bd8f403d5`. The published preparation starts with `cx q[0],q[1]`. Studio's separate 947-cycle example starts with `cx q[0],q[16]` and has no phase analysis. [Native reproduction](https://www.qccd.academy/reproduce/moses2023/) · [Studio source binding](https://github.com/yezhuoyang/QCCD/blob/97c94dbcb92c63cc529c451363603f1a813388cf/Reproduce/moses2023/results.json)

`compile_h2.py` compiles each hardware QASM directly with the Studio compiler on the H2 architecture, preserving barriers and all analysis gates. It runs native replay and the upstream Lean certificate checker and compares the emitted analysis pulses against the source. For each ion, the two compiled gates for Rz are an identity-angle equatorial rotation and the requested virtual Z rotation. The two gates for Ry are the requested equatorial rotation about the Y axis and a zero frame change. Batched gate operands are joined by logical ion identity, since their zone ordering can differ from source ordering.

The accepted Lean certificate checks gate-ion association, spatial co-location, transport and ordering. The upstream semantic checker reports its own R10 status separately for each phase. Its general pulse-unitary check is partial for non-Clifford 32-ion settings. The local analysis-gate comparison checks the emitted Rz and Ry factors against all 64 source angles and operands on each of the 32 circuits. Native model time remains in Studio units. [Upstream checker scope](https://github.com/yezhuoyang/QCCD/blob/97c94dbcb92c63cc529c451363603f1a813388cf/Compiler/bridge/check_cert.py)

The checked-in `h2-theta1.tsir.json` is the native θ₁ schedule. `h2-design.json` contains each setting's QASM and native SHA-256, certificate verdict, and the exact string substitutions against the θ₁ base. Every reconstructed byte string is checked against an independent compile. These edits encode only changes to the virtual Z angles and axes of their zero-amplitude equatorial pulses. The compiler version is identified by its local executable hash in the manifest.

`python -B validation/qccd/extract_h2.py` regenerates the measurement table from the pinned file. The extractor checks all source circuits, the rotation angles and the bit mapping. It preserves all 50 ordered 32-bit strings at each setting using eight hexadecimal digits per shot. The source repository reserves rights. The local table records factual measurements, conditions and hashes. [Quantinuum repository notice](https://github.com/Quantinuum/quantinuum-hardware-h2-benchmark/blob/d422a716977899d7d6603c9efadb44e89e495125/README.md)

`python -B validation/qccd/compile_h2.py` regenerates the native programs and checks from a configured QCCD Studio checkout with its compiler and Lean checker installed. Both regeneration scripts accept a local copy of the pinned shot file for offline use. Ordinary replay requires the checked measurement table and the QControlSys Lean executable.
