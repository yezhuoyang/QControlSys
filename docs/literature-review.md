# Hardware control for fault-tolerant quantum computers

Research review for QControlSys · 22 November 2026 · [Architecture and development plan](development-plan.md)

A controller can issue the right instruction and still produce the wrong physical operation. A measurement bit can reach the other board after its branch deadline. An RF tone can move every occupied tweezer in a column. An electrode waveform can reach a site whose local selection bit still holds the previous value. QControlSys should let a designer follow each of those outcomes from the program through the electronics to the device, then test a change to the design.

The evidence below comes from experimental papers, architecture proposals, protocol specifications, simulator documentation, and the team's existing artifacts. Numerical observations belong to the apparatus and measurement boundaries named alongside them. The proposed experiments and simulator models are engineering deductions from those sources.

## Classical controllers, clocks and transport

**Instruction issue and physical output have different clocks.** ARTIQ distinguishes the timestamp an experiment requests from the hardware time at which an event can still enter the real-time I/O system. An event issued late can underflow. Distributed RTIO adds synchronized satellites and transport. QubiC 2.0 provides a complementary architecture in which distributed processing cores issue pulses and act on measurement results. Together these suggest modeling instruction fetch, timestamped queues, clock-domain crossings, issue deadlines, and actual output edges. A scheduled pulse should retain both its requested and achieved time. [ARTIQ real-time I/O](https://m-labs.hk/artiq/manual/rtio.html) · [Distributed RTIO](https://github.com/m-labs/artiq/blob/master/doc/manual/drtio.rst) · [QubiC 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10333)

**Two boards and a scope provide a first system benchmark.** Xu et al. synchronize RFSoC boards and use fiber to send measurement data. Their room-temperature readout emulator makes the remote controller select a pulse sequence from two measurement bits. Figure 3 shows roughly 1.6 µs from the start pulse to the first conditional pulse when one is emitted, with 1 µs of readout and a programmed 600 ns hold. The hold accommodates about 150 ns of demodulation and 450 ns of communication. The four input patterns give a functional test alongside the timing test. The scope interval includes an explicit wait, so matching it alone cannot identify the fiber or transceiver delay. [Xu et al., listing 1 and figure 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09856v1)

**Streaming and addressable memory need different contracts.** AXI-Stream transfers a beat on a clock edge when the source and receiver both accept the transfer. Valid data must remain stable across a stall. FPGA block RAM has finite ports and configurable read/write behavior. External DRAM adds request scheduling, bank conflicts and refresh. Start with burst transfers on shared buses, explicit arbitration, finite FIFOs and memory visibility. Use an RTL trace for edge-level comparisons and Ramulator for experiments sensitive to the memory configuration. Turning memory size up should not automatically increase its service rate. [Arm AXI-Stream specification, §2.2](https://documentation-service.arm.com/static/64819f1516f0f201aa6b963c) · [AMD 7 Series FPGA Memory Resources](https://docs.amd.com/api/khub/documents/9gZGbqBxtlKXxBfkBt~lAg/content) · [Ramulator 2.0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11030)

**Simulation detail can vary by component.** SystemC TLM distinguishes loosely timed and approximately timed transactions. Ptolemy II gives a deterministic treatment of simultaneous events, including causal dependencies at a single simulated time. Those are useful precedents for advancing a long waveform in one interval while an adjacent FIFO executes individual transfers. Verilator supplies an independent way to exercise selected register-transfer-level (RTL) blocks. The simulator must define what the two timing resolutions agree on before an optimized component can replace the reference one. [SystemC TLM-2.0 language reference](https://www.accellera.org/images/downloads/standards/systemc/TLM_2_0_LRM.pdf) · [Discrete Event Modeling in Ptolemy II](https://ptolemy.berkeley.edu/publications/papers/99/deModeling/) · [Verilator](https://verilator.org/guide/latest/overview.html)

## Superconducting control and measurement

Stefanazzi et al.'s QICK implements waveform synthesis and capture with RFSoC converters and a timed processor. Its short-coax loopback measured 90 ns for the converter/interface round trip with digital features bypassed, 113 ns with frequency conversion, and 117 ns with conversion and interpolation/decimation. The reported feedback total spans 184–211 ns after including branch logic and subsequent pulse issue. These measurements give separate checks on the converter and sequencer profiles. A qubit measurement adds its own readout and integration window. [QICK, latency measurements and table II](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/2021/pub/fermilab-pub-21-472-scd.pdf)

The analog path changes what a correct digital command does. Rol et al.'s Cryoscope measured the step response of a transmon flux-control line at the chip. This gives the simulator a practical interface for importing a measured response, applying predistortion, and comparing residual distortion at the device. Heinsoo et al. measured five qubits through one multiplexed readout channel. Their 80 ns readout pulse and sub-250 ns resonator population interval are different observables. Preserve drive, resonator response, integration, discrimination and availability of the resulting bit as separate stages. Shared readout lines also establish a physical source of correlated behavior. [Cryoscope](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04818) · [Heinsoo et al. on multiplexed readout](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.07904)

**Proposed reference experiment.** Start with a superconducting-style readout emulator on board A driving a branch on board B. Then substitute a calibrated resonator/readout model, add a drive or flux line, and run conditional reset. Compare programmed and delivered envelopes, decision bits, and the onset of the corrective pulse. A small transmon model can test whether a distortion matters locally before promoting it to a reduced error model for large runs. [QubiC two-board experiment](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09856v1) · [Pulse-level simulation with QuTiP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.09902)

## Neutral atoms and acousto-optic control

In Bluvstein et al.'s logical processor, a spatial light modulator (SLM) provides static traps and two crossed acousto-optic deflectors (AODs) generate a mobile grid. Each axis receives a sum of RF tones. A tone steers a row or column, and the two tone sets determine all illuminated intersections. Empty traps still consume optical power. In this profile, row and column order is maintained during motion. Arbitrary rearrangements use transfers between mobile and static traps. The authors report characteristic free-space moves of about 200 µs. Their mid-circuit readout example uses a 500 µs image exposure and 200 µs for the selected pixel rows to reach the FPGA, followed by processing and a conditional TTL signal. These are separate targets for the trajectory and feedback models. [Bluvstein et al., Methods and Extended Data figure 3](https://eapg.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Logical_Quantum_Processor_Bluvstein_Nature.pdf)

Chiu et al. precomputed possible waveform chirps, selected segments from the current shot's image, and streamed one row into the arbitrary waveform generator (AWG) while computing the next. Their continuous reloading experiment turns waveform storage, DMA, and camera feedback into testable architectural choices. Ye et al.'s QuACK FPGA controller encodes sequence segments at 5 ns resolution and can branch on digital inputs locally. That is a sequencer resolution, independent of camera exposure or mechanical transport time. Fast-chirp optical tweezer work also shows when a simple steady frequency-to-position map breaks down through acoustic lensing. [Chiu et al., rearrangement methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12571880/) · [Ye et al., QuACK preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25456) · [Experiments on rapid AOD steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11451)

**Proposed reference experiment.** Image a partially filled static array, read an occupancy bitmap, select stored chirps, pick up atoms, move one shared row, release them, and image again. Record the RF command, actual beam motion, trap occupancy, losses, and time until the next usable image. Compare preloaded, streamed, and locally synthesized waveforms with the same target arrangement. Later run repeated stabilizer extraction with separate storage, entangling, readout, and reservoir zones, following the demonstrated neutral-atom fault-tolerance architecture. Atomique supplies an independent source of constrained move schedules for that later stage. [Zoned fault-tolerance experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12727508/) · [Atomique compiler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15123)

## QCCD transport, broadcasting and optical delivery

Pino et al. demonstrated ion transport and parallel interaction zones in a programmable QCCD. Moses et al.'s H2 racetrack demonstrated electrode broadcasting. Bowler et al. characterized an FPGA-controlled trapped-ion AWG. It used 16-bit DACs at 50 MHz and selected preloaded outputs in 40 ns in response to real-time signals. These are three different machines or instruments. Use H2 to test wiring and resource sharing, and the AWG to test conditional waveform selection in its own profile. [Pino et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01293) · [Moses et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.13.041052) · [Bowler et al.](https://www.nist.gov/publications/arbitrary-waveform-generator-quantum-information-processing-trapped-ions)

The wiring choice determines which ions a command can affect. WISE proposes distributing analog waveforms and switching them locally after loading a selection word. Delaney et al. experimentally tested site-dependent operations with cowired electrodes and two exchangeable local voltages. At 2.5 kHz exchange rate they measured mean excitation below one motional quantum in the reported axial modes. They effected the voltage exchange in software to emulate the proposed crossover switches. Keep fixed broadcasting, WISE selection, and the conditional exchange primitive as separate hardware profiles with their own timing and legal operations. [WISE proposal](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.040313) · [Delaney et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00756)

QCCD optics have a separate destination and clock path. Mordini et al. delivered light through integrated photonics to two zones 375 µm apart and transported an ion between Ramsey pulses in 200 µs. They also demonstrated simultaneous optical control in separate zones. The Helios processor illustrates a larger system with a storage ring, a junction, parallel operation regions and real-time compilation of dynamic programs. Optical delivery, electrode transport and classical decision traffic can therefore each bottleneck a schedule. [Mordini et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18056) · [Helios](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05465)

**Proposed reference experiment.** Send a waveform identifier and timed selection bits over a digital link to a remote board with a waveform bank. Its DAC drives cowired electrode groups. A separate laser, modulator, fiber and optical switch deliver a gate pulse after arrival. Delay one selection update across its latch edge. Inspect which electrodes received the waveform, which ions moved, and which site the gate illuminated. This is a proposed composite bench. Its constituent experiments have published precedents, but its end-to-end timing requires its own calibration.

## Fault-tolerant feedback

Two experiments isolate the quantities a control designer needs. Google Quantum AI reported a 1.1 µs syndrome cycle and average decoding latency of 63 µs for a distance-five surface code, tested up to a million cycles. The paper measures that latency from the decoder software receiving the final cycle to returning a correction. Caune et al. measured 9.6 µs from final extraction to logical feedback for their nine-round experiment, including 6.5 µs of decoding and 3.1 µs of communication and control. Averaging either result into a single per-gate cost would discard the information needed to decide when a dependent pulse may fire. [Google Quantum AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13687) · [Caune et al., results and timing definitions](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73331-6)

Run two workloads. In a memory experiment, measure syndrome production and service rates, queue occupancy and its growth over time. In a feed-forward experiment, force a subsequent physical operation to depend on the decoded result, then measure the critical path through transport, computation and actuation. A Pauli-frame update can often be deferred until consumed. Atom loss, missing readout, detection error and controller delay must have distinct event types so that a decoder can receive erasure flags where the device actually supplies them. Stim can generate or check compatible stabilizer workloads with sampled noise. The control simulation supplies timing, buffer occupancy and controller stalls. [Stim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02202) · [Neutral-atom fault-tolerance experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12727508/)

## Evidence still needed

Three measurements would most improve a first release. Obtain a simultaneous transmitter/receiver capture with timestamps and framing for a digital fiber link. Obtain an AOD tone command with a measured spot trajectory and an occupancy image under that same calibration. Obtain the command, switch latch and electrode output for one QCCD broadcast. Published aggregate timings can constrain each model, but many combinations of component delays fit the same aggregate. Store the capture boundaries, firmware, sample clock and operating conditions so a second trace can test the fitted model. Until then, expose the unknowns as parameter ranges and report which design rankings change across those ranges.
