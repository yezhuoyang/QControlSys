What can a machine ask a qubit to do?

Start with two photographs of the same experiment. By the end you can point to three different jobs in the pictures: holding an atom, changing the information it carries, and finding out what happened.

Read the picture before the labels

In panel b each small spot marks an atom seen by a camera. Imagine sliding two cups apart with a marble in each. A photograph can tell you where each marble ended up. What observation could tell you what happened to the information inside an atom?

One object, two possible answers

An atom has distinct internal energy levels. Choose two long-lived levels and give them the names 0 and 1. This pair forms a qubit. A measurement in this pair returns one of the two labels. Between measurements its state has amplitudes for both answers, including a relative phase. Phase describes how the two contributions line up when another pulse brings them together.

Prepare the same initial state many times. Apply a short burst of light each time, then count how often 1 occurs. Changing the burst can change that fraction. A reproducible transformation of the state is a gate. A measurement that reads out the result completes a trial, often called a shot.

Who does which job?

In the moving-atom experiment one beam holds an atom in place, other beams drive internal transitions, and collected light forms the image. In an electrical quantum chip a cable delivers a microwave burst to a small circuit. Both machines need a classical controller that sets the burst and records when it happened. Panel c checks what the camera positions alone could never show. The team varies the phase of a last pulse and counts joint outcomes.

Which observation tests something beyond the atoms’ final positions?

  1. Counting occupied spots in one image
  2. Changing the final pulse phase and comparing joint outcomes over many shots
The second observation is sensitive to the relative phase of the prepared pair.

What to remember

A qubit has an address in space and a state that carries information. A successful move can preserve the address assignment yet disturb the state.
