How does a pulse become a gate?

A burst can move a qubit all the way from 0 to 1, halfway, or back where it started. Use a swing to reason about frequency, then use two pulses to reason about phase.

Push in rhythm

Imagine giving a swing a small push each time it returns. Pushes timed to its natural rhythm accumulate. A qubit also has a preferred frequency given by the energy difference between its two levels. A field oscillating near this frequency can transfer amplitude between them. On resonance with a fixed phase, an isolated two-level model gives a rotation angle equal to the integral of the drive strength in radians per second over time in seconds.

A π rotation swaps 0 and 1. Half that area gives a π/2 rotation. Starting from 0, two identical π/2 pulses give 1. Shift the second pulse by half a carrier cycle and it rotates about the opposite axis, returning the ideal state to 0. This pair can reveal a phase error that a single half-pulse measurement misses.

Choose the field for the object

A transmon is a cold electrical circuit whose two lowest energy levels can hold a qubit. An oscillating voltage supplies a microwave electric field. Atoms and ions can instead absorb light on an optical transition. For two long-lived levels close in energy, two laser beams can drive a Raman transition: the difference between their frequencies supplies the level spacing. Their relative phase sets the rotation axis. The trapping beam and the gate beams serve different purposes.

Make one qubit matter to another

In the atom array, light couples one internal level to a highly excited Rydberg state. One excited atom shifts the energy of a nearby atom. A calibrated pulse sequence can therefore give a pair a phase that depends on their joint state. In an ion trap a laser force can couple both ions to a shared vibration. Returning that vibration to its start can leave a joint phase on the ions. On Manila, a microwave tone applied through one qubit near its neighbour’s frequency produces a conditional interaction. What to remember

Frequency selects a transition, pulse area sets the angle in the resonant model, and phase chooses the axis. A two-qubit gate also needs an interaction.
