Decode two clues about three qubits

You have three stored bits and permission to ask whether neighbours agree. Use just those two clues to locate one flip, then see why real decoders need a model of what can go wrong.

Ask about agreement

Encode a classical bit as 000 or 111. Form two check bits, left XOR middle and middle XOR right. A check gives 1 when its pair disagrees. Quantum error-correcting circuits use auxiliary qubits to measure analogous joint properties without directly reading each data qubit. The pattern of check results is a syndrome.

Fill all four cases

With perfect checks and at most one data-bit flip, syndrome 00 requests no correction, 10 identifies the left bit, 11 the middle bit and 01 the right bit (left check written first). A decoder maps the clues to a proposed correction. Program the AND table in F2 and feed it the two checks. The captured output implements the middle-bit flag. Two simultaneous flips can produce exactly the same syndrome as a different single flip.

Choose what changes next

A physical correction plays a pulse. A Pauli frame records a known correction in classical memory so later operations or readout can account for it. Run the QuantumOS repetition example with both checks equal to 1 and find the conditional pulse. Its other branches demonstrate frame updates. Run the three-bit lookup.

What to remember

Checks reveal relationships. The decoder’s answer depends on the assumed errors. A subsequent control action can be a pulse or a change in classical bookkeeping.
