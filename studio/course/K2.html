How a decoder grows across a chip

An error can leave clues separated in space or time. Follow the work of matching those clues, then compare two implementations of the Helios algorithm.

Put the clues on a graph

Repeated checks can themselves be noisy. A change between successive results marks a detection event. Draw possible single faults as edges between the events they might cause or between an event and a boundary. A chain of faults can leave clues only at its ends. A decoder seeks a plausible explanation from the graph.

Grow and merge neighbours

In a union-find decoder, groups around unresolved events grow and join when their growing regions meet. A group with an odd number of events needs another partner or a suitable boundary. Helios maps local work to parallel processing elements, with neighbour links and logic for coordinating progress. What to remember

A useful decoder check specifies the input syndrome, expected output and implementation. Latency needs its own endpoints, clock and hardware evidence.
