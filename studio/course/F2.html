Capture one bit on a clock edge

A detector result may change while another circuit is reading it. Build one bit of memory, change an input between clock edges, and predict which level reaches the output.

Two doors make one register

Call the table result D. When the clock is low the first storage node, called the master, follows D and the second holds its old value. When the clock rises the first closes and the second opens onto the captured master level. While the clock is high, further changes at D cannot pass both doors. This rising-edge storage element is a flip-flop. A group holding a word is a register.

Change a pin and step the circuit

The three digits in a row are clock, B, A. Step from 001 to 101: the clock rises with the XOR result equal to 1. Advance to 111. D falls while Q still holds 1. Inspect Q’s causes and then the disconnected master switches. Edit the four table bits to 0001 and rerun the same pins. Can you find the first step where Q differs?

What determines the timing on a board?


A real flip-flop needs its input stable for a short time before and after the edge, called setup and hold. Violating that window can leave the output briefly undecided. Here pin changes and edges occupy separate steps, all switches settle within a step, and Q initially holds zero. There is no conversion from those steps to nanoseconds.
