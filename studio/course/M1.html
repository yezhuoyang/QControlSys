Identify the actual machine

Manila was a five-qubit Falcon processor, revision 5.11, segment L. IBM’s archived description lays the qubits in a line, with a two-qubit command available in either direction on each adjacent pair. Select a qubit in the cutaway to read its archived properties and units. Connections follow the archive. Heights, channel positions and distances in this view are schematic. Step through the published commands

The first call, sx, applies a quarter-turn rotation to qubit 0. Select d0, its drive channel. The archived pulse lasts 160 sample ticks, about 35.56 ns on this backend. Inspect its two amplitude components, duration, width parameter sigma, and derivative correction beta. The next call, cx, acts on an ordered pair. Find its simultaneous drive and control pulses. Finally select acquire0 and find where sampling begins. The archive specifies which memory slots receive data.

What the archive lets you reproduce


The envelope plot evaluates the shape at the centre of each sample tick and applies the channel’s accumulated phase. Select a drive during a pulse to see its two components. Amplitude is normalized. Measuring volts at the chip would require the gain and response of the analog path. Qiskit pulse definitions and sampling convention.


Native channel assignments, sample offsets, pulse parameters, grouped acquisitions and phase updates come directly from the pinned command definitions. The timing and amplitudes specify a control request. The internal controller wiring, analog transfer functions, individual shot results and current device behaviour cannot be determined from that request.
