Hold one atom in an optical tweezer

What keeps an atom inside a spot of light even when no gate is playing? Follow the force across the focus and predict what happens if the focus starts moving.

Find the bottom of the well

The electric field of light induces a small oscillating dipole in a neutral atom. When the light is suitably red-detuned, the atom has lower potential energy where the light is intense. Near the centre of a tightly focused beam that energy rises in every direction away from the focus. Like a marble near the bottom of a bowl, a displaced atom can oscillate around the centre.

Transfer between a fixed and a mobile trap

Bluvstein et al. prepare an array in stationary spots. Additional movable spots pick up selected atoms. The reported beam waists are about 900 nm for the static traps and 1,000 nm for the movable ones. The waist measures the width of a beam. It is different from the distance between neighbouring qubits. The team uses a camera to equalize tweezer intensities along the transport path because changing intensity can also change the qubit phase. Bluvstein et al., Methods.

What to remember

A tweezer traps an atom with a gradient of optical intensity. A controlled transfer sets both the position and the depth of the moving well.
