How do you carry a qubit across a chip?

Two laboratories need distant partners to meet. Follow the moving trap in each apparatus, identify what stays still, then decide what evidence would show a successful journey.

Move the bottom of an electric well

An ion is an atom with electric charge. Metal patches called electrodes set up an electric field. Think of a marble at the bottom of a bowl. Varying several electrode voltages in order slides the bottom of the electrical bowl along the track while the metal itself stays put. An oscillating field helps confine the ions across the track. H2 brings selected ions to zones where laser beams can act on them.

Move the bright spot

A neutral atom has balanced positive and negative charge. Light slightly displaces those charges. In a suitable laser field this makes the atom seek the brightest part of a focused spot. That spot is an optical tweezer. Steer the spot and the atom can follow. A sudden change can leave an atom oscillating or eject it. The H2 team coordinates electrode voltages. The moving-atom team coordinates beam positions and intensities.
