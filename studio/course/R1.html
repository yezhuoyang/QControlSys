Sample a returning voltage

A readout cable brings back a changing voltage. Learn how it becomes a list of numbers and why one number rarely identifies the state of a qubit.

Take snapshots of height

The incoming line ends at an analog-to-digital converter (ADC). Like reading a ruler at regular instants, it assigns each input to one of a finite set of codes. A saturated input reaches an end code and loses information about the excess. Noise can shift a reading across a code boundary.

Listen to a resonator

A resonator stores an oscillating field, much as a bell rings near one pitch. Coupling a transmon to a resonator shifts the resonator response depending on the qubit state. Send a weak probe tone, then sample its return. The state changes the amplitude or phase of a wave. A single sample near a zero crossing can look the same for different waves.

Heinsoo et al. read five superconducting qubits through a common feedline by assigning distinct frequencies. A receiver can pick one tone out of the composite waveform. What to remember

Sampling gives voltage codes at known times. Their rate, range and timing determine what information reaches the receiver.
